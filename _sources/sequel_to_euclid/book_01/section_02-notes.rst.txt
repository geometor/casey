Book I, Section II Notes: Exercises Commentary
==============================================

.. rubric:: Historical Provenance of the Book I Exercises

1. The Incentre and Excentres in Euclidean Pedagogy
---------------------------------------------------

In Euclid's *Elements*, the incircle construction appears only in Book IV (``IV.4``). By placing the concurrence of internal and external angle bisectors among the exercises to Book I (Ex. 8–9, p. 17), Casey demonstrates that the concurrency of the angle bisectors is a purely linear consequence of angle congruence (Euclid ``I.26``) that requires neither circles nor proportions.

2. Fagnano's Orthic Perimeter Problem (1775)
--------------------------------------------

Exercise 31 (p. 19)—stating that the orthic triangle has minimal perimeter among all inscribed triangles—originates with Giovanni Fagnano (1775), who proved it by differential calculus. Hermann Amandus Schwarz (1870) gave the classical synthetic reflection proof using repeated reflections across the three sides.

3. Vector Properties of the Mean Centre
---------------------------------------

Exercises 33–34 and 45–46 (pp. 19–20) establish the fundamental projection identity of the centre of mean position: for any system of points with weights $\sum m_i \vec{P A_i} = (\sum m_i) \vec{P O}$. This formulation directly anticipates modern vector affine geometry and Möbius's barycentric calculus.
