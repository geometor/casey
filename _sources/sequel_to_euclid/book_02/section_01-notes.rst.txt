Book II, Section I Notes: Historical Provenance & Cross-Repository Commentary
=============================================================================

.. rubric:: Quadratic Geometric Algebra and Links to ``pappus``, ``altshiller-court``, and ``euler``

1. Apollonius's Median Theorem & Pappus VII.122
-----------------------------------------------

Although commonly attributed to :person:`Apollonius of Perga <apollonius>` (c. 262–190 BCE), the median theorem is preserved in Book VII, Lemma 122 of :person:`Pappus of Alexandria's <pappus>` *Collection* (*Synagoge*), where it serves as a foundation for loci problems. In ``geometor/altshiller-court`` (:cite:`*College Geometry* <altshiller-court:1952>`, § 68), Altshiller-Court classifies it as the cornerstone of metric triangle geometry.

2. Euler's Quadrilateral Identity (1748)
----------------------------------------

Leonhard Euler published Proposition 4 in *Novi Commentarii Academiae Petropolitanae* (1748), demonstrating that the departure of an arbitrary quadrilateral from the parallelogram law ($AC^2 + BD^2 = \sum s_i^2$) is precisely measured by $4\,EF^2$, where $EF$ is the segment connecting the diagonal midpoints.

3. Matthew Stewart's Cevian Theorem (1746)
------------------------------------------

Matthew Stewart, professor of mathematics at Edinburgh, published Proposition 7 in *Some General Theorems of Considerable Use in the Higher Parts of Mathematics* (1746, Proposition II). Stewart's theorem generalizes Apollonius's theorem from midpoints ($m = n = a/2$) to arbitrary cevian ratios $m : n$.

4. Leibniz's Theorem on Quadratic Moments (1677)
------------------------------------------------

Proposition 10—stating that $\sum m_i PA_i^2 = \sum m_i OA_i^2 + (\sum m_i) PO^2$—was discovered by Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz in 1677 and represents the geometric counterpart to Huygens's parallel axis theorem (Steiner's theorem) in rigid body dynamics.
