Book II, Section II Notes: Exercises Commentary
===============================================

.. rubric:: Historical Provenance of the Book II Exercises

.. folio:: 0053
   :page: 27

1. The Law of Cosines in Geometric Algebra
------------------------------------------

Euclid's Propositions II.12 and II.13 are the geometric precursors to the modern Law of Cosines for obtuse and acute angles. In Exercises 4 and 5 (p. 27), Casey gives the explicit algebraic evaluation when the angle is $60^\circ$ or $120^\circ$, directly bridging ancient geometric algebra with modern trigonometry.

2. Vecten Configuration & Outer Squares Flank Geometry
------------------------------------------------------

Exercise 13 (p. 28)—establishing that $d_a^2 + d_b^2 + d_c^2 = 3(a^2 + b^2 + c^2)$ for the lines joining the outer corners of squares on the three sides—belongs to the family of configurations studied by French mathematician Vecten (c. 1811) in Gergonne's *Annales*. The centres of these three squares determine the Vecten points $X(485)$ and $X(486)$ in Kimberling's ETC.

3. Golden Section & Fibonacci Numbers
-------------------------------------

Exercise 10 (p. 28) analyzes the metric relations of the golden section ($AB \cdot BC = AC^2$), showing that $AB^2 + BC^2 = 3 AC^2$ and $(AB + BC)^2 = 5 AC^2$. The emergence of the integer factors $3$ and $5$ reflects the Lucas number identities $L_2 = 3, L_4 = 7$ and Fibonacci relations $F_5 = 5$ governing powers of the golden ratio $\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$.
