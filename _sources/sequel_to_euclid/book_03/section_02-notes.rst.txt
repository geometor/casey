Book III — Section II: Notes
============================

.. rubric:: Historical and Mathematical Commentary on Book III Exercises

Fermat-Torricelli Point & Napoleon's Theorem
--------------------------------------------
Exercises 6–9 anticipate major 19th-century triangle discoveries:
- **Exercise 6 & 7:** Pierre de Fermat proposed to Torricelli the problem of finding a point $P$ minimizing the sum of distances to three vertices: $PA + PB + PC$. Torricelli solved it using equilateral triangles described on the sides, demonstrating concurrence of circles and connecting segments at $\angle APB = \angle BPC = \angle CPA = 120^\circ$.
- **Exercise 8 & 9:** The theorem attributing the equilateral triangle of circumcentres to Napoleon Bonaparte appeared in print in the early 19th century (e.g. Rutherford in 1825). Casey provides a direct synthetic framework for the theorem and its dual inwards-oriented counterpart.

Cyclic Quadrilaterals and Orthogonal Circles
--------------------------------------------
- **Exercise 18 & 19:** Establish the deep connection between the complete quadrilateral inscribed in a circle and orthogonal circle geometry. The circle described on the third diagonal as diameter cuts the circumscribing circle orthogonally, linking directly with Hesse's and Salmon's projective configurations.
- **Exercise 22–24:** The isoperimetric theorems for cyclic polygons, formalized by Steiner and rediscovered by modern convex geometry: among all polygons with given side lengths, the unique inscribed (cyclic) polygon maximizes enclosed area.
