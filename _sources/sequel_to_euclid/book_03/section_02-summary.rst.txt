Book III — Section II: Summary
==============================

.. rubric:: Structured Decomposition of Book III Exercises (Exercises 1–49, pp. 48–52)

Overview of Geometric Themes
----------------------------

1. **Torricelli / Fermat Point & Napoleon Triangle Configurations (Ex. 6–9):**
   - Construction of equilateral triangles on the three sides of an arbitrary triangle leads to concurrent circumcircles at the first isogonic center $X(13)$ (Fermat-Torricelli point).
   - Lines connecting original vertices to opposite external vertices are concurrent and equal in length.
   - The circumcentres form an equilateral triangle (Outer Napoleon Triangle); reversing orientation yields the Inner Napoleon Triangle, with sum of squares relation $\Sigma s_{\text{nap}}^2 = \Sigma s_{\text{orig}}^2$.

2. **Orthogonal Circles & Inscribed Quadrilaterals (Ex. 18–19, 46):**
   - The circle on the third diagonal of a cyclic quadrilateral intersects the circumscribed circle orthogonally.
   - In a semicircle with intersecting chords, the circumcircle of the upper intersection triangle cuts the semicircle orthogonally.

3. **Poles, Polars & Tangential Loci (Ex. 25, 31–34, 47–48):**
   - Loci defined by angle bisectors of polar lines and harmonic intervals.
   - Casey-Salmon contact chord properties and triads of concurrent common tangents between three circles (Monge-d'Alembert configuration).

4. **Extremum & Optimization Problems (Ex. 13, 14, 17, 22–24):**
   - Maximum area of cyclic polygons: of all polygons with given sides, the cyclic polygon possesses maximal area; for an inscribed triangle in a given circle, the equilateral triangle is maximal.
