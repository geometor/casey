Book IV, Section I Notes: Historical & Mathematical Commentary
==============================================================

.. rubric:: Historical Provenance, Inversion Geometry, and the Nine-Points Circle

.. folio:: 0079
   :page: 53

1. Lineage of the Nine-Points Circle (Brianchon–Poncelet 1821 & Feuerbach 1822)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Although :person:`Leonhard Euler <euler>` (1765) showed that the circumcentre, centroid, and orthocentre are collinear and that the circle through the altitude feet passes through the side midpoints, the explicit concyclicity of the full nine points was first published jointly by :person:`Charles Julien Brianchon <brianchon>` and :person:`Jean-Victor Poncelet <poncelet>` in Gergonne's *Annales de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées* **11** (1820–1821).

Independently, :person:`Karl Wilhelm Feuerbach <feuerbach>` published his landmark monograph *Eigenschaften einiger merkwürdigen Punkte des geradlinigen Dreiecks* (Nürnberg, 1822), establishing not only the nine-point property but the celebrated theorem that this circle touches internally the inscribed circle and externally the three escribed circles.

2. Casey's Inversive Proof of Feuerbach's Theorem
-------------------------------------------------

In Euclid's *Elements*, inversion is completely absent. :person:`John Casey <casey>` introduces inversion in Book III (Prop. 20) and brilliantly applies it in Book IV, Prop. 7 to prove Feuerbach's theorem synthetically:
1. By constructing the orthogonal circle centred at the base midpoint $D$ with radius $\frac{1}{2}(b+c)$, the two excircles touching the base produced are self-inverse.
2. The fourth common tangent to these two excircles is shown to invert directly into the Nine-Points Circle.
3. Because contact is preserved under circle inversion, the Nine-Points Circle must touch both excircles at the inverse images of the tangent points.

This proof provides an exquisite bridge between classical synthetic geometry and 19th-century transformation theory.

3. Barycentric Centres of Mean Position
---------------------------------------

Proposition 9 establishes that the incentre $O$ is the centre of mean position of vertices $A, B, C$ with weights proportional to the side lengths $a, b, c$. In homogeneous barycentric coordinates:

$$I = (a : b : c), \qquad I_a = (-a : b : c), \qquad I_b = (a : -b : c), \qquad I_c = (a : b : -c).$$

Casey's proof using signed distances perpendicular to angle bisectors demonstrates the unity of statical mechanics (Möbius's *Barycentrische Calcul*, 1827) and Euclidean geometry.
