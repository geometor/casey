Book VI, Section VI Summary: Theory of Anharmonic Section
=========================================================

.. rubric:: Mathematical Synthesis of Casey's Book VI, Section VI (pp. 126–140)

1. Cross-Ratio, Projection & Concyclic Points
---------------------------------------------

Section VI develops the projective foundations of modern synthetic geometry:

* **Anharmonic (Cross) Ratio:** For four collinear points $A, B, C, D$, the six cross-ratios formed by pairs of opposite segments satisfy the anharmonic group $\{ \lambda, \frac{1}{\lambda}, 1-\lambda, \frac{1}{1-\lambda}, \frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}, \frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1} \}$.
* **Projective Invariance (Props. 1–2):** The cross-ratio of any four collinear points is preserved under central projection from a vertex $O$. The anharmonic ratio of a pencil of four rays $O(ABCD)$ is independent of the intersecting transversal line.
* **Concyclic Points & The Butterfly Theorem (Prop. 2 Cors. 4–5):** If $A, B, C, D$ lie on a circle, the pencil $P(ABCD)$ from any variable point $P$ on the circle has a constant cross-ratio. As an immediate corollary, Casey derives the celebrated **Butterfly Theorem**: if through the midpoint $O$ of chord $AB$ two chords $CE, DF$ are drawn, the joins $ED, CF$ meet $AB$ at equal distances from $O$ ($OG = OH$).
* **Pascal's Mystic Hexagram (Prop. 4):** For any hexagon $ABCDEF$ inscribed in a circle, the intersections of opposite sides $L = AB \cap DE$, $N = BC \cap EF$, $M = CD \cap FA$ are collinear on the **Pascal Line**. Six points on a circle generate $60$ distinct Pascal lines (**Hexagrammum Mysticum**). Cor. 3 establishes **Pappus's Hexagon Theorem** when the six points lie on two straight lines.
* **Desargues's Homology Theorem (Prop. 5 Cor. 2):** Two triangles are coperspective from a centre of homology $O$ if and only if their corresponding sides meet in three collinear points on an axis of homology.

2. Involution, Homography & Classical Constructions
---------------------------------------------------

* **Involution of Points (Prop. 6 & Def.):** Six collinear points form an involution when $OA \cdot OA' = OB \cdot OB' = OC \cdot OC' = \pm k^2$. The two double points $D, D'$ satisfy $OD^2 = OD'^2 = k^2$, and each conjugate pair $(A, A')$ is harmonic with respect to $(D, D')$.
* **Desargues's Involution Theorems (Props. 7–8):**
  * Any transversal cutting three coaxal circles is cut in involution.
  * Any transversal cutting the four sides and two diagonals of a complete quadrilateral is cut in involution.
  * A transversal cutting a circle and an inscribed quadrilateral is cut in involution.
* **Homographic Ranges & Double Points (Props. 9–10):** Given two homographic ranges, the double points are constructed synthetically as the intersections of a coaxial circle with the line, or via Pascal's line on a circle.
* **Projective Solutions to Classical Problems (Prop. 11):**
  * **Castillon's Problem:** Inscribing in a polygon another polygon whose sides pass through given points.
  * **Apollonius' Problem:** Inscribing a circle tangent to three given circles using homographic pencils and double points.
* **Nine-Points Tangency Cross-Ratios (Exercise 5):** The contact points of the nine-points circle with the incircle and excircles have cross-ratios $\frac{a^2-b^2}{a^2-c^2}, \frac{b^2-c^2}{b^2-a^2}, \frac{c^2-a^2}{c^2-b^2}$.
