Book VI, Section VII Notes: Poles, Polars & Reciprocation
=========================================================

.. rubric:: Historical Context & Projective Foundations

.. folio:: 0167
   :page: 141

1. Historical Foundations: Poncelet, Chasles & Brianchon
--------------------------------------------------------

* **Philippe de La Hire (1685):** First systematic treatment of harmonic poles and polars for conic sections in *Sectiones Conicae*.
* **Jean-Victor Poncelet (1822):** In *Traité des propriétés projectives des figures*, Poncelet developed the principle of duality and the method of reciprocal polars, transforming theorems concerning points and lines into reciprocal theorems concerning lines and points.
* **Charles-Julien Brianchon (1806):** In *Sur les surfaces de courbure et les lignes de courbure*, Brianchon published his celebrated dual of Pascal's Hexagrammum Mysticum (1639), showing that opposite diagonals of a circumscribed hexagon meet in a point. Casey provides the direct polar reciprocation proof via Pascal's theorem on folios 0172–0173.
* **Michel Chasles (1837):** In *Aperçu historique sur l'origine et le développement des méthodes en géométrie*, Chasles demonstrated the power of anharmonic ratios and homographic systems on conics, which Casey systematically adapts into Euclidean elementary propositions.

2. Cross-Canon Links (:mod:`geometor.casey.poles_polars`)
---------------------------------------------------------

* **Projective Duality:** Casey's Section VII supplies the metric-to-projective bridge whereby inscribed Pascal configurations dualize via $(x_0 - h)(x - h) + (y_0 - k)(y - k) = r^2$ into circumscribed Brianchon hexagons.
* **Self-Conjugate Systems:** The self-conjugate triangle with respect to a circle provides the fundamental projective frame for polar coordinates and orthogonal involution pencils.
