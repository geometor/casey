SECTION V.
----------

APPLICATIONS — BROCARD'S CIRCLE, BROCARD'S FIRST TRIANGLE & FEUERBACH'S THEOREM.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   **Section V, Proposition 1 (:term:`The Seven-Point Brocard Circle <brocard-circle>` & Brocard's First Triangle).** *Construct the circle* :g:`(Ml O) <0220.g1:( Ml O )>` *having the segment joining the Circumcentre* :g:`O <0220.g1:O>` *and Symmedian Point* :g:`K <0220.g1:K>` *as diameter (centered at* :g:`Ml <0220.g1:Ml>` $= X(182)$ *of radius* $\frac{1}{2}|OK| = \frac{1}{2} R \sqrt{1 - 3\tan^2\omega}$*). Let the pairs of Brocard rays* :g:`[B Om1] <0220.g1:[ B Om1 ]>` *and* :g:`[C Om2] <0220.g1:[ C Om2 ]>` *meet at* :g:`A1 <0220.g1:A1>`, :g:`[C Om1] <0220.g1:[ C Om1 ]>` *and* :g:`[A Om2] <0220.g1:[ A Om2 ]>` *at* :g:`B1 <0220.g1:B1>`, *and* :g:`[A Om1] <0220.g1:[ A Om1 ]>` *and* :g:`[B Om2] <0220.g1:[ B Om2 ]>` *at* :g:`C1 <0220.g1:C1>`, *forming* **Brocard's First Triangle** :g:`△A1B1C1 <0220.g1:< A1 B1 C1 >>`. *Then:*

   1. *All* **seven points** :g:`O <0220.g1:O>`, :g:`K <0220.g1:K>`, :g:`Ω <0220.g1:Om1>`, :g:`Ω′ <0220.g1:Om2>`, :g:`A1 <0220.g1:A1>`, :g:`B1 <0220.g1:B1>`, :g:`C1 <0220.g1:C1>` *lie concyclically upon the Brocard Circle* :g:`(Ml O) <0220.g1:( Ml O )>`;
   2. :g:`Ω <0220.g1:Om1>` *and* :g:`Ω′ <0220.g1:Om2>` *are symmetric across the diameter* :g:`[O K] <0220.g1:[ O K ]>` *with* $\angle \Omega O K = \angle \Omega' O K = \omega$ *and* $\angle O \Omega K = \angle O \Omega' K = 90^\circ$;
   3. *Brocard's First Triangle* :g:`△A1B1C1 <0220.g1:< A1 B1 C1 >>` *is inversely similar to* :g:`△ABC <0220.g1:< A B C >>` *with similarity ratio* $\sin \omega$ *and shares the* **same Centroid** $G = X(2)$ *as* $\triangle ABC$!

.. folio:: 0221
   :page: 195

*Brocard's First Triangle* is inversely similar to $ABC$.

**Dem.**—Since $OA'$ is perpendicular to $BC$, and $OB'$ to $AC$, the angle $A'OB'$ is equal to $ACB$; but (Euclid ``III.21``) $A'OB'$ is equal to $A'C'B'$. Hence $\triangle A'B'C'$ is inversely similar to $\triangle ABC$, and :person:`Steiner's <steiner>` point :g:`St <0220.g1:St>` ($X(99)$) and :person:`Tarry's <tarry>` point :g:`Tr <0220.g1:Tr>` ($X(98)$) arise as the antipodal perspective pair on the circumcircle :g:`(O A) <0220.g1:( O A )>` associated with $\triangle A_1 B_1 C_1$.

**Section V, Application to the Nine-Points Circle & :person:`Feuerbach's <feuerbach>` Theorem (ff. ``0227–0229``, pp. 201–203).** Taking the three similar triangles $AB'C'$, $A'BC'$, $A'B'C$ formed inside $\triangle ABC$ by its medial triangle $\triangle A'B'C'$ as three directly similar figures of Section IV, their three pairwise centres of similitude are the side midpoints $A', B', C'$, their three invariable points are the midpoints $A'', B'', C''$ of $AH, BH, CH$, and the three altitude feet $A''', B''', C'''$ are the concurrences of three triads of homologous perpendiculars—proving that the **Circle of Similitude** of $AB'C', A'BC', A'B'C$ IS the **Nine-Points Circle** of $\triangle ABC$!

.. folio:: 0228
   :page: 202

.. enunciation::

   **Proposition 5° (Similar-Figures Proof of Feuerbach's Theorem, pp. 201–203).** *The lines joining the points $A'', B'', C''$ to the centres of the inscribed circles of the triangles $AB'C', A'BC', A'B'C$ pass through the point of contact $F$ of the nine-points circle of $ABC$ with its inscribed circle.*

   :caption: Archival woodcut (1888, f. 0228, p. 202) — Supplementary Chapter, Sec. V, Prop. 5°: Casey's similar-figures proof of Feuerbach's Theorem via the Nine-Points Circle as Circle of Similitude.

.. folio:: 0229
   :page: 203

**Dem. (Conclusion).**—Drawing $IF \parallel EA''$, the similarity ratio $r : R :: II' : DI$ fixes $IF = r$; because $EA''$ and $IF$ are parallel radii of the nine-points circle and the incircle of $\triangle ABC$, line $FA''$ passes through their internal centre of similitude $F$, proving tangency at $F$ (and likewise external tangency with every excircle whose centre $J'$ maps through the corresponding excentral rays). Q.E.D.
