Supplementary Chapter, Section VI Summary: Theory of Harmonic Polygons
======================================================================

1. Projective & Inversive Geometry of Harmonic $n$-Gons
--------------------------------------------------------

Section VI records :person:`John Casey's <casey>` original 1886 generalization of Lemoine–Brocard geometry from $n = 3$ triangles to arbitrary cyclic $n$-gons:

* **Inversive Definition:** A cyclic polygon is **harmonic** iff there exists a centre of inversion mapping its $n$ vertices onto the vertices of a regular $n$-gon (equivalently, preserving equal cross-ratios between cyclic chords and possessing an interior point $K$ whose side-distances satisfy $d_i \propto a_i$).
* **Harmonic Polar Identity (f. ``0236``):** Every transversal through $K$ intersects the $n$ side lines at $R_1, \dots, R_n$ whose harmonic center $P$ ($\sum_{i=1}^n 1/KR_i = n/KP$) traces the polar of $K$ with respect to the circumcircle.

2. Complete Transfer of Triangle Circles
----------------------------------------

Because every harmonic polygon is projectively equivalent to a regular polygon via $K$, it inherits an exact pair of Brocard points $\Omega, \Omega'$, a Brocard circle on $[OK]$, a triplicate-ratio Lemoine circle, and a one-parameter Tucker family.
