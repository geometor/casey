Unit 5 Notes: Lemoine's 1873 Revival & the English Reception via Casey
======================================================================

.. rubric:: Historical Provenance & Cross-Canon Concordance (`etc`, `altshiller-court`)

1. From Lhuilier & Grebe to Lemoine (1873) and d'Ocagne's "Symmedian"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Although the minimum-square property of $K = X(6)$ had been noticed in passing by Simon Lhuilier (1809) and Ernst Wilhelm Grebe (1847—hence the German name *Grebescher Punkt*), modern triangle geometry began in earnest at the **1873 Lyon Congress** of the *Association Française pour l'Avancement des Sciences*, where :person:`Émile Lemoine <lemoine>` presented :cite:`*Note sur un point remarquable du plan d'un triangle* <lemoine:1873>`, discovering both six-point circles (`(Ml D1)` and `(K Cb2)`). Ten years later, Maurice d'Ocagne (*Nouvelles Annales de Mathématiques*, 1883) coined the enduring French term *symédiane* ("symmedian"), while :person:`Robert Tucker <tucker>` (:cite:`QJM 19, 1883 <tucker:1883>`) rediscovered Lemoine's First Circle in England under the name **Triplicate-Ratio Circle** before recognizing it as a member of the full one-parameter Tucker family (treated in :ref:`Unit 6 <sequel-unit-6-en>`).

2. Casey's Pivotal Role in the English-Speaking World
-----------------------------------------------------

When :person:`John Casey <casey>` added his 58-page *Supplementary Chapter on the Recent Elementary Geometry of the Triangle* to the 4th edition of the *Sequel* in 1886 (expanded further in the 1888 5th and 1892 6th editions), it was the **very first textbook in any language** to organize the discoveries of Lemoine, Brocard, Tucker, Taylor, Neuberg, and Tarry into systematic Euclidean propositions! :person:`Nathan Altshiller-Court's <altshiller-court>` Chapter X (*Recent Geometry of the Triangle*, §§ 557–590, 616–628, 661–673 in ``geometor/altshiller-court``) descends directly from Casey's architecture, and ``geometor/etc`` indexes Lemoine's point as ``X(6)`` and the First Lemoine / Brocard centre as ``X(182)``.
