Book VI, Section I Notes: From Menelaus & Ceva to Modern Projective Geometry
============================================================================

.. rubric:: Historical Provenance & Cross-Repository Links to ``geometor/pappus``, ``altshiller-court``, and ``etc``

.. folio:: 0093
   :page: 67

1. Ancient Roots & Modern Revival (Menelaus & Ceva)
---------------------------------------------------

The duality between concurrency and collinearity in Section I bridges ancient Alexandrian spherical geometry to modern European synthetic geometry:

* **Menelaus of Alexandria (c. 100 CE):** Menelaus proved the transversal theorem for spherical triangles in Book III of his *Sphaerica*. The plane version was preserved in the Islamic world (notably in Thābit ibn Qurra and al-Bīrūnī) and transmitted to the West through :person:`Pappus of Alexandria's <pappus>` *Synagoge* (modeled symbolically in ``geometor/pappus``).
* **Giovanni Ceva (1678):** In *De lineis rectis se invicem secantibus statica constructio* (Milan, 1678), Giovanni Ceva rediscovered Menelaus's plane theorem and discovered the dual concurrency criterion using static moments and centres of gravity.
* **Apollonius of Perga (c. 200 BCE):** The locus of vertices with given ratio of distances (Prop. 7) is the famous *Circle of Apollonius*, known from his lost treatise *On Plane Loci*.

2. Concordance with Altshiller-Court (`geometor/altshiller-court`)
------------------------------------------------------------------

Casey's Section I corresponds directly to :cite:`*College Geometry* <altshiller-court:1952>` Chapter VI (*Transversals: Menelaus, Ceva & Desargues*, §§ 306–344). Altshiller-Court adopts Casey's exact approach: establishing Menelaus's theorem first via perpendiculars to the transversal, then proving Ceva's theorem via triangle area ratios, and applying them immediately to the isogonal, symmedian, Gergonne, and Nagel concurrencies.

3. Collation with the 1892 Sixth Edition
----------------------------------------

In :person:`Patrick A. E. Dowling's <dowling>` 1892 edition, Book VI Section I maintains verbatim alignment with Casey's 1888 text, retaining the 15 propositions and problem solutions without structural changes.
