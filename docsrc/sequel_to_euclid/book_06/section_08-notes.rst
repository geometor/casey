Book VI, Section VIII Notes: Miscellaneous Exercises
====================================================

.. rubric:: Historical Context & Advanced Invariants

1. Historical Context: Steiner, Weill & Graham
----------------------------------------------

* **Jakob Steiner (1826):** Jakob Steiner published his famous porism on closed chains of tangent circles between two non-concentric boundary circles in *Crelle's Journal* (1826). The metric criterion linking radii $R, r$, distance $\delta$, and winding numbers $(m, n)$ appears in Exercise 135.
* **M. Weill (1878) & John Casey (1862):** In Exercise 141, Casey notes that Weill's theorem on the mean centre of contact points of bicentric polygons (*Liouville's Journal*, 1878) was anticipated by Casey himself sixteen years earlier in the *Quarterly Journal of Pure and Applied Mathematics* (1862).
* **Robert Graham, T.C.D.:** A colleague of Casey at Trinity College Dublin, Graham contributed Exercises 139 and 140 on harmonic cuts of tangents by circumscribed trapezoids and coaxal touches of variable chords.

2. Pedagogical Function in Casey's Sequel
-----------------------------------------

Section VIII concludes Book VI not as mere drill problems, but as a dense research monograph that bridges Euclidean plane geometry with the nineteenth-century Dublin and Parisian schools of synthetic and projective geometry. The exercises systematically prepare the reader for the *Supplementary Chapter* on the modern geometry of the triangle (Lemoine, Brocard, and Tucker).
