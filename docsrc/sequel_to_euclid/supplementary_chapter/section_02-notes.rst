1. Rediscovery of the Brocard Configuration (1816–1881)
-------------------------------------------------------

Although August Leopold Crelle (1816) and Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi (1825) briefly encountered the equi-angle points $\Omega, \Omega'$, the systematic revival began with :person:`Henri Brocard <brocard>` in the *Nouvelle Correspondance Mathématique* (1876–1879) and his landmark 1881 Algiers memoir (:cite:`AFAS Alger, 1881 <brocard:1881>`). Casey derives both points directly from his Section II theory of **two figures directly similar** (ff. ``0199–0203``), turning spiral similarity into a pure Euclidean tool.

2. Bicentric Pairs in ``geometor/etc`` & ``geometor/altshiller-court``
----------------------------------------------------------------------

In :person:`Clark Kimberling's <casey>` *Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers* (:cite:`ETC <kimberling:etc>`, ``geometor/etc``), because $\Omega$ and $\Omega'$ swap roles under orientation reversal ($B \leftrightarrow C$) rather than remaining individually invariant, $(\Omega, \Omega')$ form the **prototype bicentric pair** (whereas their symmetric midpoint is ``X(39)`` and symmedian point is ``X(6)``). In ``geometor/altshiller-court`` (:cite:`*College Geometry*, Ch. X <altshiller-court:1952>`), Altshiller-Court follows Casey's direct-similarity construction verbatim.
