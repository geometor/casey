SECTION III.
------------

SYSTEM OF THREE SIMILAR FIGURES — LEMOINE'S, COSINE, TUCKER'S & TAYLOR'S CIRCLES.
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. enunciation::

   **Section III, Proposition 1 (:term:`Lemoine's First Circle / Triplicate-Ratio Circle <lemoine-circle>`).** *If through the Symmedian Point* :g:`K <0205.g1:K>` *three straight lines* :g:`[parBC] <0205.g1:[ parBC ]>`, :g:`[parCA] <0205.g1:[ parCA ]>`, :g:`[parAB] <0205.g1:[ parAB ]>` *be drawn parallel respectively to the sides* :g:`[BC] <0205.g1:[ B C ]>`, :g:`[CA] <0205.g1:[ C A ]>`, :g:`[AB] <0205.g1:[ A B ]>`, *meeting the sides in six points* :g:`D1 <0205.g1:D1>`, :g:`D2 <0205.g1:D2>` *on* $BC$, :g:`E1 <0205.g1:E1>`, :g:`E2 <0205.g1:E2>` *on* $CA$, *and* :g:`F1 <0205.g1:F1>`, :g:`F2 <0205.g1:F2>` *on* $AB$:

   1. *All six points lie upon a single circle—Lemoine's First Circle* :g:`(Ml D1) <0205.g1:( Ml D1 )>`;
   2. *Its centre* :g:`Ml <0205.g1:Ml>` ($X(182)$) *is the midpoint of the segment joining the Circumcentre* :g:`O <0205.g1:O>` *and Symmedian Point* :g:`K <0205.g1:K>`;
   3. *The three chords* $|D_1 D_2|, |E_1 E_2|, |F_1 F_2|$ *cut off upon the sides* $BC, CA, AB$ *are directly proportional to the* **cubes** *of those sides* ($a^3 : b^3 : c^3$, *hence :person:`Tucker's <tucker>` name "Triplicate-Ratio Circle").*

.. picture:: 0205.g1.png
   :caption: Archival woodcut (1888, f. 0205, p. 179) — Supplementary Chapter, Sec. III, Prop. 1: Lemoine's First (Triplicate-Ratio) Circle through six parallel intercepts.

.. picture:: 0205.g1.svg
   :caption: Calibrated 5-layer .euclid-graphic SVG — Supplementary Chapter, Sec. III, Prop. 1: Lemoine's First Circle (Ml D1) & Cosine Circle (K Cb2).

**Demonstration.** In parallelogram $A F_2 K E_1$ ($K E_1 \parallel AB$ and $K F_2 \parallel AC$), the diagonal $AK$ lies along the symmedian :g:`[A K] <0205.g1:[ A K ]>`. Because the adjacent sides of $A F_2 K E_1$ are proportional to the distances from $K$ to $AC, AB$ divided by $\sin A$—hence proportional to $b : c$—triangle $\triangle A F_2 E_1$ is inversely similar to $\triangle ABC$, so the chord $E_1 F_2$ is **antiparallel** to $BC$! Meanwhile $E_2 F_1$ passes through $K$ **parallel** to $BC$; hence $F_2 F_1 E_2 E_1$ is an isosceles-angle cyclic trapezium, making $E_1, E_2, F_1, F_2$ concyclic. By cyclic symmetry, $F_1, F_2, D_1, D_2$ and $D_1, D_2, E_1, E_2$ are each concyclic; since any two of these three circles share two points on one side and would otherwise have three non-concurrent radical chords $BC, CA, AB$, all six points $D_1, D_2, E_1, E_2, F_1, F_2$ lie on a single circle :g:`(Ml D1) <0205.g1:( Ml D1 )>`. Moreover, the perpendicular bisectors of the three antiparallel chords $D_2 E_1, E_2 F_1, F_2 D_1$ bisect segment $OK$, fixing the centre at :g:`Ml <0205.g1:Ml>` $= \operatorname{midpoint}(O, K)$. Finally, since $K = (a^2 : b^2 : c^2)$, the parallel intercepts cut off middle segment $|D_1 D_2| = \frac{a^3}{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$, proportional to $a^3 : b^3 : c^3$. Q.E.D.

.. enunciation::

   **Section III, Propositions 2–3 (:person:`Tucker's <tucker>` :term:`Circles <tucker-and-taylor-circles>` & The Cosine Circle).**

   1. **Tucker's Six-Point Family:** *If a triangle $\triangle A'B'C'$ be homothetic to $\triangle ABC$ with homothetic centre at the Symmedian Point* :g:`K <0205.g1:K>`, *the six intersections of the sides of $\triangle A'B'C'$ (produced) with the non-corresponding sides of $\triangle ABC$ lie upon a single circle—a Tucker Circle—whose centre lies on line $[O K]$ and whose antiparallel chords subtend constant angle with $OK$.*
   2. **Special Tucker Circles:** *The Circumcircle* :g:`(O A) <0205.g1:( O A )>`, *Lemoine's First Circle* :g:`(Ml D1) <0205.g1:( Ml D1 )>`, *and the Cosine Circle* :g:`(K Cb2) <0205.g1:( K Cb2 )>` *are all special members of the Tucker family.*

.. folio:: 0209
   :page: 183

.. porism::

   **Proposition 3 (:term:`The Cosine Circle / Second Lemoine Circle <cosine-circle>`).** *If through* :g:`K <0205.g1:K>` *three straight lines be drawn* **antiparallel** *respectively to $BC, CA, AB$, each is bisected at* :g:`K <0205.g1:K>` *and all three antiparallel segments have equal length! Consequently their six extremities on the sides of $\triangle ABC$ lie on a single circle* :g:`(K Cb2) <0205.g1:( K Cb2 )>` *centered at* :g:`K <0205.g1:K>` *itself, cutting off chords on $BC, CA, AB$ proportional to $\cos A : \cos B : \cos C$ (hence called the Cosine Circle).*

.. enunciation::

   **Section III, Proposition 4 (:person:`Taylor's <taylor>` :term:`Six-Point Circle <tucker-and-taylor-circles>`, 1882).** *If $A', B', C'$ be the vertices of the orthocentric triangle of $ABC$, and $K, N; K', N'; K'', N''$ their projections on the sides, these projections are concyclic.*

**Dem.**—Let $H$ be the orthocentre. Then the figures $AKA'N, AC'HB'$ are homothetic, $A$ being the homothetic centre. Hence $KN$ is parallel to $C'B'$, and evidently $K'K''$ is antiparallel to $C'B'$; $\therefore K'K''$ is antiparallel to $KN$. Hence the points $K, K', K'', N$ are concyclic. Similarly $K', K'', K, N'$ are concyclic. Hence the six projections lie on a single Tucker circle—Taylor's Circle—discussed in England by H. M. Taylor (*Proc. London Math. Soc.*, vol. xv., p. 122).
