==================================================================================

1. The Circle of Similitude & Invariable Points
-----------------------------------------------

Section IV develops **Casey and Neuberg's abstract theory of three directly similar plane figures** $F_1, F_2, F_3$:

* **Triangle & Circle of Similitude:** The three pairwise spiral-similarity double points $S_1, S_2, S_3$ define the circumcircle $(S_1 S_2 S_3)$, known as the **Circle of Similitude** of the triad.
* **Locus of Co-intersecting Symmedians (f. ``0216``):** For every homologous triangle $\triangle D_1 D_2 D_3$ bounded by three corresponding lines $d_1, d_2, d_3$, the joins $S_1 D_1, S_2 D_2, S_3 D_3$ concur at a single point $K$ tracing $(S_1 S_2 S_3)$ whose distances to $d_1, d_2, d_3$ are proportional to the side lengths $(a_1 : a_2 : a_3)$.

   :page: 190

2. Structural Role in Casey's Architecture
------------------------------------------

Section IV acts as the master engine behind Section V: by specializing $F_1, F_2, F_3$ to three congruent/similar triangles constructed on the sides of $\triangle ABC$, the abstract Circle of Similitude immediately becomes the **Brocard Circle** or the **Nine-Points Circle**, yielding unified geometric proofs of Brocard's First Triangle and Feuerbach's Theorem.
