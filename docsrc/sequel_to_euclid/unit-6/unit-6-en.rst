Section II — Figures Directly Similar, Brocard Points $\Omega, \Omega'$ & Brocard Angle $\omega$
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Theory of Two Figures Directly Similar (Props. 1–2).** Given any two directly similar plane figures, there exists a unique **Double (Self-Corresponding) Point**—the Centre of Spiral Similarity—about which a rotation through a fixed angle combined with a constant homothecy carries the first figure onto the second.

.. folio:: 0202
   :page: 176

.. enunciation::

   **Section II, Propositions 3–5 (:person:`Brocard's <brocard>` :term:`Points <brocard-points>` $\Omega, \Omega'$ & :term:`Brocard Angle <brocard-angle>` $\omega$).** *In any triangle* :g:`△ABC <0220.g1:< A B C >>`:

   1. *The three circles described on* $AB$ *tangent to* $BC$ *at* $B$, *on* $BC$ *tangent to* $CA$ *at* $C$, *and on* $CA$ *tangent to* $AB$ *at* $A$ *concur at a unique interior point* :g:`Ω <0220.g1:Om1>` (*the First Brocard Point*) *such that*

      $$
      \angle \Omega AB = \angle \Omega BC = \angle \Omega CA = \omega.
      $$

   2. *Cyclically reversing orientation, the three circles tangent at* $A, B, C$ *in the opposite sense concur at the Second Brocard Point* :g:`Ω′ <0220.g1:Om2>` (*isogonal conjugate of* :g:`Ω <0220.g1:Om1>`) *with the* **same** *common acute angle* $\angle \Omega' BA = \angle \Omega' CB = \angle \Omega' AC = \omega$.
   3. *The Brocard Angle* $\omega$ *satisfies the fundamental cotangent and cosecant identities:*

      $$
      \cot \omega = \cot A + \cot B + \cot C = \frac{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}{4\Delta}, \qquad \csc^2 \omega = \csc^2 A + \csc^2 B + \csc^2 C,
      $$

      *with sharp upper bound* $\omega \le 30^\circ$ *(equality iff* $\triangle ABC$ *is equilateral).*

Section III (Part 2) & Section IV — Tucker's Circles, Taylor's Circle & Three Similar Figures
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. enunciation::

   **Section III, Propositions 2–5 (:person:`Tucker's <tucker>` :term:`Circles & Taylor's Circle <tucker-and-taylor-circles>`).**

   1. **Tucker's Six-Point Family:** *If a triangle* $\triangle A'B'C'$ *be homothetic to* $\triangle ABC$ *with homothetic centre at the Symmedian Point* :g:`K <0220.g1:K>`, *the six intersections of the sides of* $\triangle A'B'C'$ *(produced) with the non-corresponding sides of* $\triangle ABC$ *lie upon a single circle—a Tucker Circle—whose centre lies on line* :g:`[O K] <0220.g1:[ O K ]>` *and whose antiparallel chords subtend constant angle with* $OK$.
   2. **Special Tucker Circles:** *The Circumcircle* :g:`(O A) <0220.g1:( O A )>`, *Lemoine's First Circle* :g:`(Ml D1) <0220.g1:( Ml D1 )>`, *and the Cosine Circle are all special members of the Tucker family.*
   3. **:person:`Taylor's <taylor>` Circle (1882):** *Let* $D_a, D_b, D_c$ *be the feet of the altitudes of* $\triangle ABC$, *and drop perpendiculars from each altitude foot upon the other two sides of the triangle. The six feet of these perpendiculars lie upon a single Tucker circle—Taylor's Circle—whose centre is the incentre of the medial-orthic antiparallel triangle!*

.. folio:: 0213
   :page: 187

**Section IV — General Theory of Three Similar Figures.** Extending Section II to three directly similar figures constructed homologously upon the sides of $\triangle ABC$, their three pairwise centres of similarity lie on their **Circle of Similitude**, and homologous lines bound variable triangles constantly inversely similar, collapsing at a minimum-area configuration linked directly to :g:`Ω <0220.g1:Om1>`, :g:`Ω′ <0220.g1:Om2>`, and :g:`K <0220.g1:K>`.

Section V — Brocard's Circle, Brocard's First Triangle & Second Proof of Feuerbach's Theorem
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. enunciation::

   **Section V, Propositions 1–3 (:term:`The Seven-Point Brocard Circle <brocard-circle>` & Brocard's First Triangle).** *Construct the circle* :g:`(Ml O) <0220.g1:( Ml O )>` *having the segment joining the Circumcentre* :g:`O <0220.g1:O>` *and Symmedian Point* :g:`K <0220.g1:K>` *as diameter (centered at* :g:`Ml <0220.g1:Ml>` $= X(182)$ *of radius* $\frac{1}{2}|OK| = \frac{1}{2} R \sqrt{1 - 3\tan^2\omega}$*). Let the pairs of Brocard rays* :g:`[B Om1] <0220.g1:[ B Om1 ]>` *and* :g:`[C Om2] <0220.g1:[ C Om2 ]>` *meet at* :g:`A1 <0220.g1:A1>`, :g:`[C Om1] <0220.g1:[ C Om1 ]>` *and* :g:`[A Om2] <0220.g1:[ A Om2 ]>` *at* :g:`B1 <0220.g1:B1>`, *and* :g:`[A Om1] <0220.g1:[ A Om1 ]>` *and* :g:`[B Om2] <0220.g1:[ B Om2 ]>` *at* :g:`C1 <0220.g1:C1>`, *forming* **Brocard's First Triangle** :g:`△A1B1C1 <0220.g1:< A1 B1 C1 >>`. *Then:*

   1. *All* **seven points** :g:`O <0220.g1:O>`, :g:`K <0220.g1:K>`, :g:`Ω <0220.g1:Om1>`, :g:`Ω′ <0220.g1:Om2>`, :g:`A1 <0220.g1:A1>`, :g:`B1 <0220.g1:B1>`, :g:`C1 <0220.g1:C1>` *lie concyclically upon the Brocard Circle* :g:`(Ml O) <0220.g1:( Ml O )>`;
   2. :g:`Ω <0220.g1:Om1>` *and* :g:`Ω′ <0220.g1:Om2>` *are symmetric across the diameter* :g:`[O K] <0220.g1:[ O K ]>` *with* $\angle \Omega O K = \angle \Omega' O K = \omega$ *and* $\angle O \Omega K = \angle O \Omega' K = 90^\circ$;
   3. *Brocard's First Triangle* :g:`△A1B1C1 <0220.g1:< A1 B1 C1 >>` *is inversely similar to* :g:`△ABC <0220.g1:< A B C >>` *with similarity ratio* $\sin \omega$ *and shares the* **same Centroid** $G = X(2)$ *as* $\triangle ABC$!

Sections VI–VII & Miscellaneous Exercises — Harmonic Polygons, Steiner, Tarry, Neuberg & Longchamps
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Section VI — Theory of :term:`Harmonic Polygons <harmonic-polygon>` (Casey, 1886; pp. 205–218).** Generalizing the symmedian geometry of a triangle (every cyclic $3$-gon is harmonic!) to cyclic $n$-gons: a cyclic polygon $A_1 A_2 \dots A_n$ is *harmonic* when it is the circular-inversion or projective image of a regular $n$-gon—equivalently, when there exists an interior Lemoine point $K$ whose perpendicular distances to the $n$ sides are proportional to the side lengths, generating generalized Brocard points, Lemoine circles, and Cosine circles for any $n \ge 4$.

.. folio:: 0245
   :page: 219

.. enunciation::

   **Section VII & Miscellaneous Exercises (:person:`Steiner's <steiner>` Point $X(99)$, :person:`Tarry's <tarry>` Point $X(98)$, :person:`Neuberg's <neuberg>` Circles & :person:`de Longchamps' <longchamps>` Point $X(20)$).**

   1. **:term:`Steiner's Point <steiner-and-tarry-points>` (:g:`St <0220.g1:St>` $= X(99)$):** *Straight lines drawn through the vertices* :g:`A <0220.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0220.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0220.g1:C>` *parallel respectively to the sides* $B_1 C_1, C_1 A_1, A_1 B_1$ *of Brocard's First Triangle concur at a single point* :g:`St <0220.g1:St>` *lying on the Circumcircle* :g:`(O A) <0220.g1:( O A )>` *(fourth intersection of the Circumcircle with Steiner's Circumellipse).*
   2. **:term:`Tarry's Point <steiner-and-tarry-points>` (:g:`Tr <0220.g1:Tr>` $= X(98)$):** *Perpendiculars drawn through* $A, B, C$ *to the corresponding sides* $B_1 C_1, C_1 A_1, A_1 B_1$ *of Brocard's First Triangle concur at* :g:`Tr <0220.g1:Tr>` *on the Circumcircle—diametrically opposite to* :g:`St <0220.g1:St>` *so that* $\operatorname{midpoint}(S_t, T_r) = O$!
   3. **Neuberg's Circles:** *The locus of the vertex* $P$ *of a variable triangle* $\triangle PBC$ *on fixed base* $BC$ *having the same Brocard angle* $\omega$ *as* $\triangle ABC$ *is a circle (an $A$-Neuberg Circle) orthogonal to the circle on $BC$.*
   4. **de Longchamps' Point & Circle (Misc. Ex. 146–158, p. 247):** *The reflection* $L = 2 O - H = X(20)$ *of the Orthocentre* $H$ *across the Circumcentre* $O$ *on the Euler Line is the Orthocentre of the anticomplementary triangle and centre of Longchamps' radical circle.*
