Book I, Section II: Exercises¶

A Sequel to the First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid (5th ed., 1888, pp. 17–20)

f. 0043 · p. 17

SECTION II.¶

Exercises.¶

  1. The line which bisects the vertical $\angle$ of an isosceles $\triangle$ bisects the base perpendicularly.

  2. The diagonals of a quadrilateral whose four sides are equal bisect each other perpendicularly.

  3. If the line which bisects the vertical $\angle$ of a $\triangle$ also bisects the base, the $\triangle$ is isosceles.

  4. From a given point in one of the sides of a $\triangle$ draw a right line bisecting the area of the $\triangle$.

  5. The sum of the $\perp$s from any point in the base of an isosceles $\triangle$ on the equal sides is = to the $\perp$ from one of the base angles on the opposite side.

  6. If the point be taken in the base produced, prove that the difference of the $\perp$s on the equal sides is = to the $\perp$ from one of the base angles on the opposite side; and show that, having regard to the convention respecting the signs plus and minus, this theorem is a case of the last.

  7. If the base $BC$ of a $\triangle$ be produced to $D$, the $\angle$ between the bisectors of the $\angle$s $ABC, ACD = \text{half } \angle BAC$.

  8. The bisectors of the three internal angles of a $\triangle$ are concurrent.

  9. Any two external bisectors and the third internal bisector of the angles of a $\triangle$ are concurrent.

  10. The quadrilaterals formed either by the four external or the four internal bisectors of the angles of any quadrilateral have their opposite $\angle\text{s} = \text{two right } \angle\text{s}$.

f. 0045 · p. 19

  1. The lines joining the feet of the $\perp$s of a $\triangle$ form an inscribed $\triangle$ whose perimeter is a minimum.

  2. If from the extremities $A, B$ of the base of a $\triangle ABC$ $\perp$s $AD, BE$ be drawn to the opposite sides, prove that

$$ AB^2 = AC \cdot AE + BC \cdot BD. $$

  1. If $A, B, C, D$, &c., be any number ($n$) of points on a line, and $O$ their centre of mean position; then, if $P$ be any other point on the line,

$$ AP + BP + CP + DP + \text{&c.} = n OP. $$

  1. If $O, O'$ be the centres of mean position for two systems of collinear points, $A, B, C, D$, &c., $A', B', C', D'$, &c., each system having the same number ($n$) of points; then

$$ n OO' = AA' + BB' + CC' + DD' + \text{&c.} $$

  1. If $G$ be the point of intersection of the bisectors of the $\angle$s $A, B$ of a $\triangle$, right-angled at $C$, and $GD$ a $\perp$ on $AB$; then, the rectangle $AD \cdot DB = \text{area of the } \triangle$.

  2. The sides $AB, AC$ of a $\triangle$ are bisected in $D, E$; $CD, BE$ intersect in $F$: prove $\triangle BFC = \text{quadrilateral } ADFE$.

f. 0046 · p. 20

  1. If $AB, CD$ be opposite sides of a $\Box$, $P$ any point in its plane; then $\triangle PBC = \text{sum or difference of the } \triangle\text{s } CDP, ACP$, according as $P$ is outside or between the lines $AC$ and $BD$.

  2. If equilateral $\triangle$s be described on the sides of a right-angled $\triangle$ whose hypotenuse is given in magnitude and position, the locus of the middle point of the line joining their vertices is a $\odot$.

  3. If $CD$ be a $\perp$ on the base $AB$ of a right-angled $\triangle ABC$, and if $E, F$ be the centres of the $\odot$s inscribed in the $\triangle$s $ACD, BCD$, and if $EG, FH$ be lines through $E$ and $F \parallel$ to $CD$, meeting $AC, BC$ in $G, H$; then $CG = CH$.

  4. If $A, B, C, D$, &c., be any system of collinear points, $O$ their mean centre for the system of multiples $a, b, c, d$, &c.; then, if $P$ be any other point in the line,

$$ (a + b + c + d + \text{&c.}) OP = a \cdot AP + b \cdot BP + c \cdot CP + d \cdot DP + \text{&c.} $$

  1. If $O, O'$ be the mean centres of the two systems of points $A, B, C, D$, &c., $A', B', C', D'$, &c., on the same line $L$, for a common system of multiples $a, b, c, d$, &c.; then

$$ (a + b + c + d + \text{&c.}) OO' = a \cdot AA' + b \cdot BB' + c \cdot CC' + d \cdot DD' + \text{&c.} $$
