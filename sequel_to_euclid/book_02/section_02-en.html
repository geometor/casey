Book II, Section II: Exercises¶

A Sequel to the First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid (5th ed., 1888, pp. 27–28)

f. 0053 · p. 27

SECTION II.¶

Exercises.¶

  1. The second and third Propositions of the Second Book are special cases of the First.

  2. Prove the fourth Proposition by the second and third.

  3. Prove the sixth by the fifth, and the tenth by the ninth.

  4. If the $\angle C$ of a $\triangle ACB$ be $\frac{1}{3}$ of two right $\angle$s ($60^\circ$), prove

$$ AB^2 = AC^2 + CB^2 - AC \cdot CB. $$

  1. If $C$ be $\frac{2}{3}$ of two right $\angle$s ($120^\circ$), prove

$$ AB^2 = AC^2 + CB^2 + AC \cdot CB. $$

f. 0054 · p. 28

  1. In a quadrilateral the sum of the squares of two opposite sides, together with the sum of the squares of the diagonals, is equal to the sum of the squares of the two remaining sides, together with four times the square of the line joining their middle points.

  2. Divide a given line $AB$ in $C$, so that the rectangle under $BC$ and a given line may be equal to the square of $AC$.

  3. Being given the rectangle contained by two lines, and the difference of their squares: construct them.

  4. Produce a given line $AB$ to $C$, so that $AC \cdot CB$ is equal to the square of another given line.

  5. If a line $AB$ be divided in $C$, so that $AB \cdot BC = AC^2$, prove $AB^2 + BC^2 = 3 AC^2$, and $(AB + BC)^2 = 5 AC^2$.

  6. In the fig. of Prop. xi. prove that—

    (1). The lines $GB, DF, AK$, are parallel. (2). The square of the diameter of the $\odot$ about the $\triangle FHK = 6 HK^2$. (3). The square of the diameter of the $\odot$ about the $\triangle FHD = 6 FD^2$. (4). The square of the diameter of the $\odot$ about the $\triangle AHD = 6 AH^2$. (5). If the lines $EB, CH$ intersect in $J$, $AJ$ is $\perp$ to $CH$.

  7. If $ABC$ be an isosceles $\triangle$, and $DE \parallel$ to the base $BC$, and $BE$ joined, $BE^2 - CE^2 = BC \cdot DE$.

  8. If squares be described on the three sides of any $\triangle$, and the adjacent angular points of the squares joined, the sum of the squares of the three joining lines is equal to three times the sum of the squares of the sides of the triangle.

  9. Given the base $AB$ of a $\triangle$, both in position and magnitude, and $m AC^2 - n BC^2$: find the locus of $C$.

  10. If from a fixed point $P$ two lines $PA, PB$, at right angles to each other, cut a given $\odot$ in the points $A, B$, the locus of the middle point of $AB$ is a $\odot$.

  11. If $CD$ be any line $\parallel$ to the diameter $AB$ of a semicircle, and if $P$ be any point in $AB$, then

$$ CP^2 + PD^2 = AP^2 + PB^2. $$

  1. If $O$ be the mean centre of a system of points $A, B, C, D$, &c., for a system of multiples $a, b, c, d$, &c.; then, if $L$ and $M$ be any two $\parallel$ lines,

$$ \Sigma(a \cdot AL^2) - \Sigma(a \cdot AM^2) = \Sigma(a) \cdot (OL^2 - OM^2). $$
