Book III, Section I Notes: Historical Provenance & Cross-Repository Commentary¶

Provenance of the Radical Axis, Poncelet’s Limiting Points, and La Hire’s Reciprocity

1. Louis Gaultier de Tours & the Radical Axis (1813)¶

The concept of the Radical Axis (axe radical) and Radical Centre (centre radical) was introduced by Louis Gaultier of Tours in his 1813 memoir Les contacts des cercles (Journal de l’École Polytechnique 9, cahier 16, pp. 124–214). Gaultier showed that every pair of non-concentric circles possesses a straight-line locus of equal tangent lengths, which serves as the natural coordinate axis for coaxial pencils.

2. Jean-Victor Poncelet & Limiting Points (1822)¶

In his foundational Traité des propriétés projectives des figures (Paris, 1822, §§ 76–88), Jean-Victor Poncelet recognized that non-intersecting coaxial circle systems possess two real focal points—which he termed points limites (Limiting Points)—where circle radii shrink to zero. Poncelet demonstrated that the limiting points are inverse points with respect to every circle of the coaxial pencil, and that every circle orthogonal to the coaxial pencil passes through both limiting points!

3. Philippe de La Hire & the Principle of Reciprocity (1685)¶

Philippe de La Hire established the harmonic theory of poles and polars in his Sectiones Conicae (Paris, 1685, Lib. II). Proposition 25 encapsulates La Hire’s Reciprocal Theorem: the pole of a line connecting two points is the intersection of their polars, and the polar of the intersection of two lines is the connector of their poles. This duality is the synthetic precursor to modern projective duality.
