Book VI, Section VIII: Miscellaneous Exercises¶

A Sequel to the First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid (5th ed., 1888, pp. 148–164)

f. 0174 · p. 148

SECTION VIII.¶

MISCELLANEOUS EXERCISES.¶

  1. The lines from the angles of a $\triangle$ to the points of contact of any $\odot$ touching the three sides are concurrent.

  2. Three lines being given in position, to find a point in one of them, such that the sum of two lines drawn from it, making given angles with the other two, may be given.

  3. From a given point in the diameter of a semicircle produced to draw a line cutting the semicircle, so that the lines may have a given ratio which join the points of intersection to the extremities of the diameter.

  4. The internal and external bisectors of the vertical angle of a $\triangle$ meet the base in points which are harmonic conjugates to the extremities.

  5. The rectangle contained by the sides of a $\triangle$ is greater than the square of the internal bisector of the vertical angle by the rectangle contained by the segments of the base.

  6. State the corresponding theorem for the external bisector.

  7. Given the base and the vertical angle of a $\triangle$, find the following loci:—

    (1). Of the intersection of perpendiculars. (2). Of the centre of any circle touching the three sides. (3). Of the intersection of bisectors of sides.

  8. If a variable $\odot$ touch two fixed $\odot\text{s}$, the tangents drawn to it from the limiting points have a constant ratio.

f. 0175 · p. 149

  1. The $\perp$ from the right angle on the hypotenuse of a right-angled $\triangle$ is a harmonic mean between the segments of the hypotenuse made by the point of contact of the inscribed circle.

  2. If a line be cut harmonically by two $\odot\text{s}$, the locus of the foot of the $\perp$, let fall on it from either centre, is a $\odot$, and it cuts any two positions of itself homographically (see Prop. 3, Cor. 2, Section VII.).

  3. Through a given point to draw a line, cutting the sides of a given $\triangle$ in three points, such that the anharmonic ratio of the system, consisting of the given point and the points of section, may be given.

  4. If squares be described on the sides of a $\triangle$ and their centres joined, the area of the $\triangle$ so formed exceeds the area of the given triangle by $\frac{1}{8}\text{th}$ part of the sum of the squares.

  5. The locus of the centre of a $\odot$ bisecting the circumferences of two fixed $\odot\text{s}$ is a right line.

  6. Divide a given semicircle into two parts by a $\perp$ to the diameter, so that the diameters of the $\odot\text{s}$ described in them may be in a given ratio.

  7. The side of the square inscribed in a $\triangle$ is half the harmonic mean between the base and perpendicular.

  8. The $\odot\text{s}$ described on the three diagonals of a quadrilateral are coaxal.

  9. If $X, X'$ be the points where the bisectors of the $\angle A$ of a $\triangle$ and of its supplement meet the side $BC$, and if $Y, Y'; Z, Z'$, be points similarly determined on the sides $CA, AB$; then

$$ \frac{1}{XX'} + \frac{1}{YY'} + \frac{1}{ZZ'} = 0; $$

and

$$ \frac{a^2}{XX'} + \frac{b^2}{YY'} + \frac{c^2}{ZZ'} = 0. $$

  1. Prove Ptolemy’s Theorem, and its converse, by inversion.

  2. A line of given length slides between two fixed lines: find the locus of the intersection of the $\perp\text{s}$ to the fixed lines from the extremities of the sliding line, and of the $\perp\text{s}$ on the fixed lines from the extremities of the sliding line.

  3. If from a variable point $P$ $\perp\text{s}$ be drawn to three sides of a $\triangle$; then, if the area of the $\triangle$ formed by joining the feet of these $\perp\text{s}$ be given, the locus of $P$ is a circle.

f. 0190 · p. 164

  1. If $R, r$ be the radii of two circles, $C, C'$, of which the former is supposed to include the latter; then if a series of circles $O_1, O_2, O_3, \dots O_m$ be described touching both and touching each other in succession, prove that if traversing the space between $C, C'$ $n$ times consecutively the circle $O_m$ touch $O_1$ if $\delta$ be the distance between the centres,

$$ (R - r)^2 - 4 R r \tan^2 \frac{n \pi}{m} = \delta^2. \quad \text{—(Steiner.)} $$

  1. If $A, B, C, D$ be any four points, and if the three pairs of lines which join them intersect in the points $b, c, d$, then the nine-points circles of the four triangles $ABC, ABD, ACD, BCD$, and the circle about the triangle $bcd$, all pass through a common point $P$.

  2. If $A_1, B_1, C_1, D_1$ be the orthocentres of the triangles $A, B, C$, &c., and $b_1, c_1, d_1$ the points determined by joining $A_1, B_1, C_1, D_1$, in pairs; then the nine-points circles of the four triangles $A_1, B_1, C_1$, &c., and the circumscribed circle of the triangle $b_1 c_1 d_1$, all pass through the former point $P$.—(Ex. 29.)

  3. The Simson’s lines (Book III. Prop. 12) of the extremities of any diameter of the circumcircle of a triangle intersect at right angles on the nine-points circle of the triangle.

  4. [*] Every tangent to a circle is cut harmonically by the sides of a circumscribed square, and also by the sides of a circumscribed trapezoid whose non-parallel sides are equal.

  5. A variable chord of a circle passes through a fixed point; its extremities and the fixed point are joined to the centre; prove that the circumcircles of the three triangles so formed touch in every position a pair of circles belonging to two given coaxal systems.

  6. Weill's Theorem.—If two circles be so related that a polygon of $n$ sides can be inscribed in one and circumscribed to the other, the mean centre of the points of contact is a fixed point.

  7. In the same case the locus of the mean centre of any number $(n - r)$ of the points is a circle.

Weill’s Theorem was published in Liouville’s Journal, Third Series, Tome IV., page 270, for the year 1878. A proposition, of which Weill’s is an immediate inference, was published by the Author in 1862, in the Quarterly Journal of Pure and Applied Mathematics, Vol. V., page 44, Cor. 2.

Footnotes

[*]

Theorems 139, 140, have been communicated to the author by Robert Graham, Esq., A.M., T.C.D.
