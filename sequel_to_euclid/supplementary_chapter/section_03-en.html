SECTION III.¶

SYSTEM OF THREE SIMILAR FIGURES — LEMOINE’S, COSINE, TUCKER’S & TAYLOR’S CIRCLES.¶

Section III, Proposition 1 (:term:`Lemoine’s First Circle / Triplicate-Ratio Circle <lemoine-circle>`). If through the Symmedian Point K three straight lines [parBC], [parCA], [parAB] be drawn parallel respectively to the sides [BC], [CA], [AB], meeting the sides in six points D1, D2 on $BC$, E1, E2 on $CA$, and F1, F2 on $AB$:

  1. All six points lie upon a single circle—Lemoine’s First Circle (Ml D1);

  2. Its centre Ml ($X(182)$) is the midpoint of the segment joining the Circumcentre O and Symmedian Point K;

  3. The three chords $|D_1 D_2|, |E_1 E_2|, |F_1 F_2|$ cut off upon the sides $BC, CA, AB$ are directly proportional to the cubes of those sides ($a^3 : b^3 : c^3$, hence :person:`Tucker’s <tucker>` name “Triplicate-Ratio Circle”).

Demonstration. In parallelogram $A F_2 K E_1$ ($K E_1 \parallel AB$ and $K F_2 \parallel AC$), the diagonal $AK$ lies along the symmedian [A K]. Because the adjacent sides of $A F_2 K E_1$ are proportional to the distances from $K$ to $AC, AB$ divided by $\sin A$—hence proportional to $b : c$—triangle $\triangle A F_2 E_1$ is inversely similar to $\triangle ABC$, so the chord $E_1 F_2$ is antiparallel to $BC$! Meanwhile $E_2 F_1$ passes through $K$ parallel to $BC$; hence $F_2 F_1 E_2 E_1$ is an isosceles-angle cyclic trapezium, making $E_1, E_2, F_1, F_2$ concyclic. By cyclic symmetry, $F_1, F_2, D_1, D_2$ and $D_1, D_2, E_1, E_2$ are each concyclic; since any two of these three circles share two points on one side and would otherwise have three non-concurrent radical chords $BC, CA, AB$, all six points $D_1, D_2, E_1, E_2, F_1, F_2$ lie on a single circle (Ml D1). Moreover, the perpendicular bisectors of the three antiparallel chords $D_2 E_1, E_2 F_1, F_2 D_1$ bisect segment $OK$, fixing the centre at Ml $= \operatorname{midpoint}(O, K)$. Finally, since $K = (a^2 : b^2 : c^2)$, the parallel intercepts cut off middle segment $|D_1 D_2| = \frac{a^3}{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$, proportional to $a^3 : b^3 : c^3$. Q.E.D.

f. 0207 · p. 181

Section III, Propositions 2–3 (:person:`Tucker’s <tucker>` :term:`Circles <tucker-and-taylor-circles>` & The Cosine Circle).

  1. Tucker’s Six-Point Family: If a triangle $\triangle A'B'C'$ be homothetic to $\triangle ABC$ with homothetic centre at the Symmedian Point K, the six intersections of the sides of $\triangle A'B'C'$ (produced) with the non-corresponding sides of $\triangle ABC$ lie upon a single circle—a Tucker Circle—whose centre lies on line $[O K]$ and whose antiparallel chords subtend constant angle with $OK$.

  2. Special Tucker Circles: The Circumcircle (O A), Lemoine’s First Circle (Ml D1), and the Cosine Circle (K Cb2) are all special members of the Tucker family.

f. 0209 · p. 183

Proposition 3 (:term:`The Cosine Circle / Second Lemoine Circle <cosine-circle>`). If through K three straight lines be drawn antiparallel respectively to $BC, CA, AB$, each is bisected at K and all three antiparallel segments have equal length! Consequently their six extremities on the sides of $\triangle ABC$ lie on a single circle (K Cb2) centered at K itself, cutting off chords on $BC, CA, AB$ proportional to $\cos A : \cos B : \cos C$ (hence called the Cosine Circle).

f. 0210 · p. 184
Section III, Proposition 4 (:person:`Taylor’s <taylor>` :term:`Six-Point Circle <tucker-and-taylor-circles>`, 1882). If $A', B', C'$ be the vertices of the orthocentric triangle of $ABC$, and $K, N; K', N'; K'', N''$ their projections on the sides, these projections are concyclic.

Dem.—Let $H$ be the orthocentre. Then the figures $AKA'N, AC'HB'$ are homothetic, $A$ being the homothetic centre. Hence $KN$ is parallel to $C'B'$, and evidently $K'K''$ is antiparallel to $C'B'$; $\therefore K'K''$ is antiparallel to $KN$. Hence the points $K, K', K'', N$ are concyclic. Similarly $K', K'', K, N'$ are concyclic. Hence the six projections lie on a single Tucker circle—Taylor’s Circle—discussed in England by H. M. Taylor (Proc. London Math. Soc., vol. xv., p. 122).

f. 0213 · p. 187

The concluding corollaries (ff. 0211–0214) determine the centre of Taylor’s Circle as the incentre of the triangle formed by joining the midpoints of the sides of the orthic triangle $\triangle A'B'C'$ before turning to the general theory of three directly similar figures in Section IV.
